Creating worlds

I’m Rasmus Lundager, a multidisciplinary creative producer working where sound, story, performance and space meet.

01 / Philosophy

My philosophy

I believe the best art happens when people have room to bring themselves into the work: their lives, experiences, and heart.

I think people can feel it on the other side. Something of the person who made it reaches the person experiencing it, and the work takes on another life through them.

I’m drawn to art that leaves a feeling I can’t quite explain. A sound, an image, an atmosphere that follows me home. In my own work, I keep asking how we can give those feelings a form together.

Through my studies in Process Leadership, I explored ways to make this possible. Three principles guide me: Structure, Flow and Emergence. Structure gives us a shared foundation, flow keeps us engaged in making, and emergence allows the work to become greater than the sum of its parts.

02 / Structure

Structure

I want people to know what we’re working towards, what they’re responsible for, and where they can influence the work. Defined roles and clear ways to communicate, share decisions, and ask for help give us a dependable foundation.

Structure doesn’t necessarily mean a strong hierarchy. In my experience, knowing where you stand creates safety and makes it easier to act, contribute, and take creative risks. I try to make the practical side clear enough that people can give their attention to what we’re making. Good structure brings progress and more freedom to everyone involved.

03 / Flow

Flow

Good structure creates the conditions for flow. I think every process has a sweet spot: a pace that keeps us engaged, with enough room to think, feel, and explore. I pay attention to that rhythm. When does an idea need more time? When would trying it tell us more than another discussion? When is it time to move on?

Once we’re working, I like us to put on our “yes, let’s try it” hats. I’ve been in plenty of discussions where we could have tried everyone’s version, made a bad one for fun, and had lunch by the time we agreed how to begin. Stopping to argue every choice while we’re making can break that flow, just as people are finding their shared instincts. Seeing the alternatives tends to bring us together faster: we have something to respond to, and the best direction often becomes clear. My job is to keep the pace, energy, and intuition alive, knowing when a conversation will help and when to let us keep going.

04 / Emergence

Emergence

Good flow makes room for emergence: discoveries none of us could have imagined alone. That’s one of my favourite things about making art. A coincidence, an unexpected combination, or even a mistake can take us somewhere we would never have planned to go. Someone hears an idea differently. A sound changes the mood. We try something almost as a joke, and suddenly it makes sense. Occasionally, the joke is the best idea we’ve had all day.

I recognise emergence when people’s contributions begin changing one another and the work takes on a life of its own. These moments challenge our habits and give our creativity something unfamiliar to work with. I want to stay open to them, including the ones that ask us to rethink our original idea. Sometimes that’s how we find what makes the work special: something nobody could have thought of before we started making it together.

05 / Closing thoughts

Closing thoughts

From A to B. From idea to finished work. We tend to focus on those two points: where we begin and what we end up with. But I’ve realized what excites me the most is the work in the in-between. Because this is where the real magic happens.

I want that space to be exciting, energetic, and full of heart. A process with a good rhythm, where people feel safe enough to take chances, build confidence, and make something together.

So let’s channel our sprawling imaginations and teamwork to manifest forms to unseen worlds!
