Spatial Audio & Acoustic Architecture

Naturama 360° Sound Experience — The Taste of Sound

An octophonic subterranean listening dome where spectators are submerged within reconstructed primeval Nordic biomes. Built inside Naturama's purpose-crafted hemispherical cyclorama, the project unites directional beamforming, low-frequency kinetic bone-conduction transducers, and responsive volumetric illumination into an undivided perceptual field.

Acoustic Medium 64-Channel Ambisonics
Spatial Volume 420 m³ Acoustic Dome
Acoustic Treatment Micro-Perforated Basotect G+
Chapter 01

The Dramaturgy of Primeval Bio-Acoustics

The acoustic curation was predicated on capturing micro-auditory phenomena ordinarily imperceptible to human ear canal mechanics. Over six months of field recordings across old-growth Danish forests and Baltic maritime shelf coordinates, hydrophone arrays and ultrasonic field recorders registered the metabolic clicking of ancient lichen, low-frequency whale navigational sweeps, and subterranean fungal spore shifts.

“Sound operates here not as background atmosphere, but as an architectural physical material that rearranges physical space.”

Through Higher-Order Ambisonics, these acoustic artifacts are mapped as dynamic spatial trajectories orbiting the listener. Spectators seated at the basalt tables experience the sensations of standing underneath an unfurling canopy: raindrops are calculated individually with localized surface impacts, shifting the listener's center of balance and heightening bodily immersion.

Chapter 02

Technical Implementation & Spatial Tuning

Constructing an accurate 360-degree sonic hemisphere inside an untreated concrete dome posed severe acoustical hazards, predominantly high-frequency flutter echoes and severe focal standing waves at 120Hz. The solution demanded a bespoke hybrid architecture: sixty-four decoupled Basotect G+ acoustic sails suspended along algorithmic reflection curves, effectively reducing RT60 decay from 3.2 seconds down to an ultra-precise 0.42 seconds.

Generative Composition Engine

Rather than looping static audio files, the dome operates an autonomous Max/MSP algorithmic patch that continuously recalculates panning vectors based on real-time ambient museum temperature, visitor density sensors, and lunar tide phases. No visitor encounters an identical acoustic arrangement.

// 16 COAXIAL BEAMFORMERS // 8 INFRASOUND ACTUATORS // ZERO AUDIBLE DISTORTION

Kinetic tactile transducers embedded directly beneath the oak seating assemblies reproduce tactile frequencies between 14Hz and 35Hz. The auditory cues become haptic, allowing visitors to feel low-frequency seismic earth movements and resonant whale communication as physical vibrations through the skeletal structure.
